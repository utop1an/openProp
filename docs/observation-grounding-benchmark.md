# Observation-process grounding confirmation

## Research question

The interval-censoring benchmark shows that delayed detection biases estimated
hazards, but hazard error alone does not prove relevance to entity grounding.
This confirmation asks a narrower downstream question: can inspection frequency
become a false scene-conditioned persistence signal that changes the final
matcher ranking?

The experiment is synthetic controlled decision evidence. It is designed to
establish a mechanism and does not estimate how often the mechanism occurs in
natural scenes.

## Frozen protocol

Two scene strata have the same exponential transition hazard, 0.25 per hour,
but different inspection schedules:

| Scene stratum | Inspection interval |
|---|---:|
| `frequent-scene` | 0.5 h |
| `sparse-scene` | 4.0 h |

Each seed supplies 600 training histories per scene. The paired estimators see
the same latent histories:

- `detected-time naive` removes the interval lower bound and treats first
  detection as the transition time;
- `interval-aware` retains the last-negative/first-positive interval;
- `true-hazard oracle` receives the generator hazard and is evaluation-only.

The 40 analytic grounding cases are fixed across seeds. Every case contains two
red cups with the queried `on(cup, table)` observation. The target observation
is three hours old and the distractor observation is four hours old. Equal
latent hazards therefore imply that the target should rank first. Target scene
is balanced exactly: 20 targets are in each scene. Scene is available to the
persistence model but is not a query constraint.

The cases alternate candidate order, and the tests also reverse every candidate
tuple. `current_truth` exists only on the evaluation case and never enters entity
properties or matcher inputs. Overall Top-1 is primary; worst-target-scene Top-1
and absolute target-scene Top-1 gap are predeclared secondary metrics.

Development used seeds `101, 211, 307, 401, 503`. After the cases and metrics
were fixed, confirmation used untouched seeds
`607, 701, 809, 907, 1009, 1103, 1201, 1301, 1409, 1511`.

## Confirmation result

Values are mean and population standard deviation across ten confirmation
seeds. Paired intervals bootstrap seeds with 20,000 deterministic resamples.

| Estimator | Overall Top-1 ↑ | Worst-scene Top-1 ↑ | Target-scene gap ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Detected-time naive | 0.550 ± 0.150 | 0.100 ± 0.300 | 0.900 ± 0.300 |
| Interval-aware | **1.000 ± 0.000** | **1.000 ± 0.000** | **0.000 ± 0.000** |
| True-hazard oracle | **1.000 ± 0.000** | **1.000 ± 0.000** | **0.000 ± 0.000** |

Interval-aware minus naive overall Top-1 is **+0.450** with paired 95% CI
**[0.350, 0.500]** and 9/1/0 seed wins/ties/losses. Worst-scene Top-1 improves
by **+0.900 [0.700, 1.000]**, and the target-scene gap decreases by
**0.900 [0.700, 1.000]**. The tie is seed 701 and remains in all aggregates.

The error is directional rather than random. The naive estimator usually learns
the sparsely inspected scene as more persistent and therefore selects its older
observation when the target is in the frequently inspected scene. Balancing
target scene prevents this harm from being hidden by aggregate target
composition.

## Claim boundary

Supported: when scene strata have equal latent persistence but different
inspection intervals, detection-time training can introduce a scene-conditioned
ranking error, and interval-aware likelihood removes it on the frozen analytic
confirmation.

Not supported: natural effect prevalence, informative inspection, missed
detections, recurrent transitions, nonlinear hazards, visual grounding, or
real/semi-real longitudinal effectiveness. Those require the official TEACh
experiment and cannot be inferred from this benchmark.
